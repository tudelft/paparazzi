Autopilot Tuning Report

How the gains were found: measure first, change one thing, prove it in simulation, repeat.

The Method — no guesswork

1
Build a truthful physics model. A JSBSim flight-dynamics model of the Talon 250G was created from the real specs (250 g, 620 mm span, 6.6 dm², 5×5 pusher) and validated against known behaviour: stall 6.7 m/s, top speed 16 m/s, glide ratio ~9.
2
Derive first-guess gains from physics. Cruise throttle, climb feedforwards and limits were calculated from the model (e.g. measured 28 % throttle at 12 m/s), not copied from another plane.
3
Fly repeatable test patterns headlessly. Scripted flights — hand launch, +30 m altitude step, laps of Oval 1-2 — measured altitude error, airspeed and bank continuously, scored as capture time, dip, balloon and overshoot, split into "straights" vs "turns".
4
Change one gain, re-fly, compare numbers. Only changes that measurably improved the numbers (without breaking another test) were kept. Where no gain could fix the physics, the firmware itself was improved: a bank-angle energy feedforward and a constant-deceleration altitude capture were added to energy_ctrl.c (both default-off, upstream-safe).

Background & Engineering Notes

Why Energy Control — and What to Watch

Why (E)TECS suits a small UAV

  • One budget, two currencies. TECS treats height and speed as one energy account: E = mgh + ½mV². Throttle adds/removes energy; pitch only decides whether it is stored as height or speed. That matches how an airplane actually works.
  • Light airframes trade energy fast. A 250 g foamie converts speed↔height in a heartbeat: a classic "pitch for altitude, throttle for altitude too" controller constantly fights itself, TECS never does — each actuator has exactly one job.
  • Stall protection by construction. Speed is a first-class citizen: the controller floors its setpoint at 1.23×stall, and when the throttle saturates it deliberately gives up climb rather than airspeed. On a plane that stalls at 6.7 m/s that discipline is survival.
  • It learns the airframe ("adaptive"). The nominal cruise throttle and pitch are slowly re-estimated in flight. As the 2S Li-Ion sags from 8.4 V to 6.6 V — or a different battery shifts the CG — the trim follows automatically, so the plane flies the same at the end of the pack as at the start.
  • Wind-gust economy. A gust converts to a speed error, absorbed mostly by re-distributing energy (pitch) rather than motor bursts — smoother flight, longer endurance.
  • Feedforward-friendly. Because the energy bookkeeping is explicit, predictable costs (banked turns, climbs) can be paid in advance with physically-sized feedforwards — the feedback loops then only handle genuine surprises like gusts.
Rule of thumb worth keeping: feedback fixes what you did not foresee; feedforward pays for what you can compute. Every improvement in this campaign that actually moved the numbers was a feedforward sized from the airframe's physics — the feedback gains merely needed to be awake (integrators) and calm (no saturation).

Risks & points of attention

  • Airspeed sensor is mission-critical. ETECS is blind without it — a blocked or icy pitot means the controller chases a phantom speed (we reproduced this in sim: a missing airspeed feed caused a full-throttle dive). Pre-flight: blow-check the SDP33, watch the reading during the launch sprint.
  • On-ground adaptation poisoning. After landing, airspeed reads ~0 while the integrators are still allowed to "learn" — the cruise throttle can wind up (known airframe TODO: aircraft may throttle up after touchdown). Kill the throttle promptly at touchdown.
  • No altitude integrator. The altitude loop is P-only: any sustained disturbance leaves a standing error ~1/PGAIN in size. The high PGAIN 0.6 used here is only comfortable because the capture law removes its harshness on approaches — do not copy the value without the capture feature.
  • Capture law constraints. Keep ALTITUDE_ACCEL_CAPTURE ≤ ALTITUDE_MAX_CLIMB_DOT (or the rate limiter lags the level-off and causes overshoot) and note the sqrt branch only engages when PGAIN > CAPTURE/MAX_CLIMB — with a low PGAIN the feature silently does nothing.
  • Do not compensate twice. The bank pitch feedforward and the classic H_CTL_PITCH_OF_ROLL both raise the nose in turns. They are deliberately split here (0.19 leading / 0.04 trailing); enabling both at full size will balloon every turn.
  • Feedforwards must match reality. All were computed from the model; if the prop, weight or battery changes materially, recompute them — wrong feedforward makes every manoeuvre start with a lurch.
  • Respect saturation. MAX_ACCELERATION and ALTITUDE_MAX_CLIMB are deliberately set inside the physical envelope — "asking for more" only winds up integrators.
  • Sim-to-real gap. These gains were proven against a validated model, but a real airframe adds vibration, sensor noise and servo slop. Start the first flights with the bank feedforwards at ~70 % of the sim values and walk them up via dl_settings.

Firmware Contributions & Transfer Guide

Two New Features — and How to Tune the Next Airframe

1 · Bank-angle energy feedforward

In a coordinated turn the wing must produce lift for load factor n = 1/cosφ and induced drag grows with n² — a perfectly predictable energy cost. The feedforward pays it as the wing rolls (using the commanded bank, which leads the actual turn), in two parts: 
/* u = 1/cos^2(phi) - 1, bank capped at 60 deg */
throttle += K_steady * u          /* exact turn cost   */
         +  K_washout * highpass(u) /* roll-in punch,
    zero mid-turn, NEGATIVE on roll-out (kills
    the exit balloon). tau = 2 s              */
pitch    += K_pitch * (1/cos(phi) - 1);
  • Talon 250G values: steady 0.36, washout 0.85, τ 2.0 s, pitch 0.19.
  • Safety: bounded (60° cap), purely additive, never integrated (cannot pollute the adaptation), vanishes wings-level. Defaults to off.

2 · Constant-deceleration altitude capture

The classic proportional law levels off exponentially — the last metres crawl (17.8 s here). The new law approaches at full climb, then bleeds climb rate at a constant, chosen deceleration a, blending tangentially into the linear law for the final centimetres: 
/* outside |err| <= a/P^2 :          */
climb_sp = sign(e) * sqrt(2a(|e| - a/(2P²)))
/* inside: classic  climb_sp = P * e
   (continuous in value AND slope)     */
  • Result: level-off 4.0 s (−78 %), +0.05 m overshoot, airspeed untouched — the altitude-vs-time curve becomes a clean parabola instead of a long tail.
  • Both features live in energy_ctrl.c, documented in guidance_energy.xml, tunable in flight via dl_settings, and default off — candidates for an upstream pull request after a flight-test campaign.

Recipe: tuning another airframe

1
Measure the plane first. Weigh it. Find true stall speed, best-glide speed and ratio, top speed, max climb rate, and — crucially — the throttle fraction that holds cruise speed level. Sim or careful manual flight both work.
2
Fill in the physics, not defaults: battery voltage under load; NOMINAL_CRUISE_THROTTLE = measured; CLIMB_THROTTLE_INCREMENT = (1 − cruise)/max-climb; PITCH_OF_VZ ≈ 1/airspeed; MAX_ACCELERATION below (T−D)/W; airspeed floor at 1.23×stall.
3
Wake up the attitude loops. Verify the pitch/roll integrators actually act within seconds (compute SUM_ERR_MAX/IGAIN — if it is minutes, they are decorative), give the feedforwards (KFFA) real values, then fly straight and level hands-off before touching anything else.
4
Tune energy loops on a step. Command ±30 m: raise ENERGY_TOT P/I until throttle answers promptly without hunting, then ENERGY_DIFF P/I until speed stays pinned during the climb. Watch the airspeed trace, not the altitude trace.
5
Then turns. Fly ovals; log altitude split by bank angle. Size the steady bank-throttle gain from the dip, add washout for the entry transient, and only then trim pitch-of-bank. One knob per flight.
6
Last: capture crispness. Enable ALTITUDE_ACCEL_CAPTURE at ~0.5, raise ALTITUDE_PGAIN until the terminal region feels tight (remember: the sqrt branch needs PGAIN > a/MAX_CLIMB), and re-run the turn test — a stiffer PGAIN usually means the steady bank gain can come down.
7
Regress everything, every time. A change that helps captures can break turns (it did here, twice). Keep the scripted tests; numbers beat impressions.
The one unbreakable rule: change one parameter at a time, and keep a written (or scripted) record of the number it moved. Two knobs at once means neither is understood — and an autopilot that "mostly works" for reasons nobody knows is a future crash.
